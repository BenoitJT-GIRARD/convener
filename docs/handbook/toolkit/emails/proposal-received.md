# Email — A proposal has been received

*To whoever proposed a speaker, on the day the proposal arrives. It is very
often the first thing an outside person ever receives from this series, and
for many of them it is the only one until the board has decided.*

*Send it even when the proposal is obviously going nowhere. A researcher who
proposes somebody and hears nothing does not conclude that the answer was no;
they conclude that the series is defunct, and they do not propose again.*

---

**Subject:** Thank you — we have your proposal for the {{ instance.short_name }} {{ instance.series }}

Dear {{ proposed_by.name }},

Thank you for proposing {{ speaker.name }} for {{ instance.organisation }} Virtual
Workshop Series. Your proposal has reached us and is on the list the editorial
board reads.

**What happens next.** Our editorial board looks at every proposal against the
criteria the series publishes — the quality of the work, whether its core
question is a behavioural one, and a deliberate preference for early-career
and less visible researchers, alongside the balance of the programme as a
whole. The board votes, and the decision is recorded.

**Roughly when.** We aim to come back to you within two to three weeks. The
board is made of volunteers, so it is sometimes a little longer; if you have
heard nothing after a month, please do write and chase us. That is a welcome
message, not a nuisance.

**You will hear from us either way.** Whether the answer is yes, not now, or
no, somebody will write to you. A no is not a judgement on the researcher or
their work: we have a handful of slots a year and we balance the programme
across fields, career stages, countries and institutions, so we decline good
proposals every season. Proposals we would like to come back to are kept, and
we say so when that is what has happened.

**One thing worth knowing.** If you proposed somebody other than yourself, we
do not contact them before the board has decided. Nobody is told they were
proposed and then told they were not selected.

Thank you for thinking of the series — proposals from the community are where
most of our speakers come from.

Best regards,

{{ host_1.name }}
*for {{ instance.organisation }} team*

---

## Notes for the volunteer sending this

- Send it the day the proposal arrives, or the next one. Its value is almost
  entirely in being prompt; a warm acknowledgement three weeks later is worth
  less than a plain one on the day.
- The window this message gives is deliberately a range and not a date. The
  series holds a target for board decisions in its configuration, and it can
  change; a message that promised "by the 14th" would be a promise the record
  never made.
- The criteria are summarised here in one sentence rather than reproduced.
  They live in [how we validate speakers](../../governance/selection-criteria.md),
  which is the page to point anyone at who asks for more.
- If the proposal names the person who sent it as the speaker — someone
  proposing themselves — the message still works as written; you are writing
  to them either way.
- When the board decides, the answer goes out on
  [declined](decision-declined.md) or [parked](decision-parked.md), or the
  lead moves on to an [invitation](invitation.md). This message promises that
  one of those three arrives; make sure one does.
