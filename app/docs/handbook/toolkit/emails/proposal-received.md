# Email — A proposal has been received

*To whoever sent a proposal in, on the day it arrives, when the proposal comes
with an address to write to. Many do not: the form asks for the speaker's
details and not the sender's, so there is often nobody to write to at all, and
the step is skipped.*

*It acknowledges receipt and promises nothing else. There are far more
proposals than slots, choosing between them is genuinely difficult, and the
person the series contacts when a proposal is taken up is the speaker.*

---

**Subject:** Thank you — we have your proposal for the {{ instance.short_name }} {{ instance.series }}

Dear colleague,

Thank you for proposing {{ speaker.name }} for {{ instance.organisation }}
{{ instance.series }}. Your proposal has reached us and is on the list the
editorial board reads.

**What happens next.** Our editorial board looks at every proposal against the
criteria the series publishes — the quality of the work, whether its core
question is a behavioural one, and a deliberate preference for early-career and
less visible researchers, alongside the balance of the programme as a whole.
The board votes, and the decision is recorded.

**What we can and cannot say afterwards.** We receive many more good proposals
than we have slots. We do not write back to everyone who sends one in, so
please do not read our silence as a judgement on the researcher or their work.
Where a proposal is taken up, it is the speaker we approach.

**Please do propose again.** A season with no room for a name often has room
for it the next time round, and proposals from the community are where most of
our speakers come from.

**One thing worth knowing.** If you proposed somebody other than yourself, we
do not contact them before the board has decided. Nobody is told they were
proposed and then told they were not selected.

Thank you for thinking of the series.

Best regards,

The {{ instance.organisation }} team

---

## Notes for the volunteer sending this

- **Send it only if you have somewhere to send it.** The proposal form asks for
  the speaker's details and not the sender's, so there is frequently no address
  on the record. Leave the line unticked and carry on; nothing further down the
  journey waits on it.
- **When you can, send it the day the proposal arrives**, or the next one. Its
  value is almost entirely in being prompt; a warm acknowledgement three weeks
  later is worth less than a plain one on the day.
- **Put a name in if you know one.** *Dear colleague* is there for the ordinary
  case where the record holds no name you could use. Replacing it takes a
  second, and leaving it costs nothing.
- **The signature is the series, not a person.** No host has been named on this
  record yet — hosts are chosen once the board has approved the speaker — so
  there is no individual to sign for it.
- **Promise no more than receipt.** This message used to say that whoever
  proposed would hear back either way. The series cannot keep that: there are
  many proposals, a handful of slots, and the person contacted when one is
  taken up is the speaker. Keep anything you add inside that.
- The criteria are summarised here in one sentence rather than reproduced. They
  live in [how we validate speakers](../../governance/selection-criteria.md),
  which is the page to point anyone at who asks for more.
- If the proposal names the person who sent it as the speaker — someone
  proposing themselves — the message still works as written, and they are also
  the person the board's own answer reaches, on
  [declined](decision-declined.md) or [parked](decision-parked.md), or as an
  [invitation](invitation.md).
