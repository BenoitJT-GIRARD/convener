# Forum post — Discussion summary

*Posted after the webinar, as a reply under the announcement thread. It is a summary of the discussion — not a record of everything that was said.*

## What you write it from

Your own notes, taken during the discussion — see [Phase 4](../workflow/4-after.md) for what is worth writing down while you are also hosting. Two other things are already written and cost you nothing:

- the **forum thread**, where the questions asked before the talk are in the askers' own words;
- the **questions from the chat**, if the host watching the chat copied them across as they came.

Nothing else is provided. There is no recording of the words spoken, and none is expected: a summary written from a page of honest notes reads better than one written from everything.

## How to make it

1. **Group your notes into three or four themes.** Two questions that circle the same point belong in one paragraph, not two. Anything that came up once and went nowhere can be left out — the post is a summary, and a short one is read.
2. **Write it**, following the structure below. Aim for the length of a long forum reply, not an article.
3. **Show the draft to {{ speaker.name }} before posting it.** They will catch an answer of theirs that came out wrong, and they often add the reference they could not remember live. Give them a couple of working days; if you hear nothing back, post it — the summary is due within a week of the talk.
4. **Post it** as a reply under the announcement thread.

## What the post should contain

- One line: the talk is now on YouTube — {{ speaker.youtube_url }}.
- Three or four short paragraphs, one per theme: the question that opened it, and what {{ speaker.name }} answered.
- Anything the speaker pointed to — a paper, a dataset, a tool, a name — with a link where you have one. This is the part readers come back for.
- An invitation to keep the conversation going on the thread.

## Check before you post

- [ ] No questioner is named. Questions are summarised, never attributed — the same rule as in the room.
- [ ] Nothing is put in the speaker's mouth. If your notes do not support a sentence, cut it.
- [ ] Names, affiliations and any references are spelled correctly.
- [ ] The YouTube link opens the right video.
- [ ] {{ speaker.name }} has seen the draft.
- [ ] It reads as a forum post: plain British English, no jargon that was not used on the day.

---

## Optional — if you would like help drafting

Everything above is the whole procedure; it is complete without this section, and a summary written entirely by hand is exactly what is wanted. Some people find a first draft easier to edit than a blank page, so: if you already use an AI assistant and are happy to, you may paste your notes into it and edit what comes back. **No tool is provided, none is imposed, and nothing here needs an account, an installation or a subscription.** If that is not for you, ignore this section — no step of the procedure depends on it.

A prompt that works:

> Here are my notes from the discussion after a scientific webinar. Turn them into a short summary in British English, to post on {{ instance.organisation }} as a reply under the post that announced the talk. Say the talk is now on YouTube at {{ speaker.youtube_url }}. Group the questions into three or four themes rather than listing them; do not name anyone who asked a question; do not add anything that is not in my notes. Make it read like a forum post: give the context, be tactful, keep it short.

Whatever comes back is a draft and not the post. Read it against your notes, cut what you cannot vouch for, and run the checks above — including showing it to the speaker.
