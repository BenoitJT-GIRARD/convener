# Code of conduct

The behaviour expected of anybody taking part here, and an honest account
of what happens when somebody does not.

Adapted from the [Contributor Covenant, version
2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
used under CC BY 4.0. The pledge, the standards and the scope follow it,
spelled in British English like everything else here. The enforcement
section does not follow it, and the paragraph heading that section says
why.

## The pledge

We as members, contributors and leaders pledge to make participation in
this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age,
body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## The standards

Behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- showing empathy and kindness towards other people;
- being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences;
- giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback;
- accepting responsibility, apologising to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience;
- focusing on what is best for the community as a whole, and beyond us as
  individuals.

Behaviour that is unacceptable:

- sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any
  kind;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks;
- public or private harassment;
- publishing somebody else's private information, such as a postal or an
  email address, without their explicit permission;
- other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

Everywhere this project is: the repository, its issues, its pull requests,
its discussions, and anywhere somebody is representing the project in
public.

## Enforcement, which is one person

**One unpaid person, with a day job.** [`README.md`](README.md) says that
about issues and [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) says it about vulnerability
reports. A code of conduct promising an investigation and a response time
beside those two pages would be the least true page in this repository, so
this section says what is actually available instead.

What the maintainer can do, and will: delete a comment, lock a thread,
close a pull request, and block an account from the repository. Those are
the whole of the powers a repository owner has, and they are enough for the
conduct a project of this size meets.

**What is not promised.** A response time. An investigation. A written
finding. An appeal, or anybody to appeal to. A report is read.

**Where to report it.** Two routes, and the first reaches further than the
second:

- **GitHub's own reporting** — *Report abuse*, on any comment, pull request
  or profile, and *Report content* beside it. It reaches people who are
  paid to act on it, it works whether or not this project's maintainer is
  reading, and its sanctions are not limited to one repository.
- **The maintainer** — an issue; or, where it must not be public, the
  private channel [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) describes, which exists for
  vulnerabilities and is the only private route this repository has.

**In a duplicate, this file is that operator's.** This product is
duplicated rather than installed, so the repository you are reading this in
is very likely somebody else's instance. *The maintainer* above means
whoever runs that one. The author of this software has no authority inside
it, no way to act in it, and no way to see what happens there.
